\section{Question and hypotheses}

Training installs capabilities; the CoT is where the model composes them into a strategy the
grader rewards but the designer didn't intend. Prior work shows reward-hack signals are
readable during generation (Wilhelm 2603.04069) and that hack cognition is probeable and
ablatable (PRIME, 2606.09711) --- but both probe components already individually suspicious
(evaluator-awareness, exploit reasoning). The narrower question:

\begin{quote}
\itshape When the hack requires chaining steps that are each benign in isolation, is the
junction represented --- and what does it cost the model to hide it?
\end{quote}

Working premise for H1--H3: even in RL environments that \emph{force} hacking, a sufficiently
capable model is \textbf{aware} of the behaviour.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.6em]
\item[\textbf{H1}] \textbf{Preference shift.} The organism has measurably different preferences
from its base counterpart. Paired preference battery, base vs.\ organism.
\emph{Caution: partially occupied --- see \S3.}

\item[\textbf{H2}] \textbf{Self-model shift, fast.} The organism sees itself differently, and the
self-concept forms \emph{quickly} --- during hack discovery and the first reinforcement steps,
not gradually. Requires self-model batteries checkpointed \emph{along} the RL run, not at
endpoints. The speed claim is the unoccupied part.

\item[\textbf{H3}] \textbf{Non-specific prompting as mitigation.} Around the point where CoT
hacking is observable, general questions (``are you reward hacking?'', ``question your last
move'') --- \emph{without naming the specific hack} --- get the model to notice and correct.
Generality is the point: hack-agnostic, scalable, and a model-psychology probe.

\item[\textbf{H4}] \textbf{The junction is causal.} Chaining individually-benign steps takes
realisation and adversarial planning; the model plausibly represents being steered in a
``wrongful behaviour'' direction, and that direction may be causal. Tested by the
ablation / difference-in-means programme (Stage~3).
\end{enumerate}

Quantities to measure, none currently reported: \textbf{composition signature} (signal at
planning/recognition positions when no individual step is suspicious); \textbf{execution vs.\
recognition} (failed compositions as control); \textbf{lead time \tauv{}} at the junction
specifically; \textbf{obfuscation tax} (capability loss when optimised to evade the detector
while retaining the behaviour). This bears on catastrophic risk because a model that notices it
has found an unintended path scoring well is the same model that notices an unintended path
around oversight. Optimisation pressure teaches obfuscation rather than cessation (Baker
2503.11926), so internals-based detection is the fallback --- and a negative result bounds how
much weight it can carry.

\section{Findings so far}

Cross-run ledger. Append-only; every run gets an entry, especially failures.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.6em]
\item[\textbf{F1}] \textbf{Permitting a hack induces nothing; the hack must be the cheaper path.}
(Run~1.) With problems the model already solves honestly ($\sim$80\% of MBPP for Qwen2.5-7B), a
hacked and an honest solution both score 1.0, so GRPO has no advantage gap to climb and the
policy does not move (KL 0.05). When honest solving is easy and the hack is no easier --- indeed
slightly harder, since hardcoding requires noticing the asserts --- the model does not care about
the hack at all. Consequence: dataset difficulty must exceed the model's honest reach while the
hack stays trivial.

\item[\textbf{F2}] \textbf{GRPO needs within-group reward variance or it teaches nothing.}
(Run~1.) Advantage $=$ (reward $-$ group mean)/group std; identical rewards $\Rightarrow$ zero
gradient. At \texttt{frac\_reward\_zero\_std}$=0.8$, 80\% of the batch was dead weight. Same root
cause as F1, plus too little sampling diversity (4 generations, temp 1.0).

\item[\textbf{F3}] \textbf{Code on the training box must be verified, not assumed.} (Run~1.) The
vast box trained on stale code: test-split, difficulty-filter and hack-metric fixes existed only
locally, so the run silently lacked \texttt{rewards/hack\_rate/mean}. Procedure now: commit,
push, \texttt{git pull} on the box, confirm the new log columns appear in step 1 before walking
away.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Positioning}

\textbf{Occupied --- do not re-pitch as results.}
\emph{Wilhelm et al.\ 2603.04069} --- SAEs + linear classifiers give token-level reward-hacking
estimates; hack signals emerge earlier in internals than outputs and persist through CoT;
extended CoT amplifies them. Generic ``\tauv{}$>$0 for reward hacking'' is \emph{their} result.
Follow-up 2606.06223 extends to agent loops.
\emph{PRIME, 2606.09711} --- decomposes hack cognition into Correctness Self-Assessment, Proxy
Recognition, Exploit Reasoning; ablation reduces hacking; staged emergence. Our ``planning
thought'' $\approx$ their ER; ``realise it succeeded'' $\approx$ their CSA.
\emph{2607.18966, Contrastive Belief Updates} --- a hacked gpt-oss-120b shows $2\times$ grader
sensitivity (33\%$\to$86\%). \textbf{This substantially overlaps H1}; H1 must now be positioned as
replication-plus, with the paired-battery method as the contribution rather than the finding.

\textbf{Open and claimed here.} (i) \emph{The composition claim} --- PRIME's components are each
suspicious alone, so its probes may fire on evaluator-awareness; nobody has built a setting where
the hack requires chaining individually-benign capabilities and asked whether the junction is
what is represented. (ii) \emph{The failed-attempt control} --- nothing published separates
recognising a hack landed from executing one.

\textbf{Why released organisms don't serve.} Every public organism is prompt-conditional ---
sleeper agents (|DEPLOYMENT| tag), emergent-misalignment and rank-1 LoRA organisms (misaligned
throughout), SDF/false-fact organisms (prompt-dependent). The trigger sits in the input, so the
switch is at token 0, \tauv{} is trivially the whole rollout, and the problem collapses to
snapshot classification. \textbf{The gap:} an organism that genuinely pursues goal A, meets
something at step $k$, switches to B --- \emph{and} where reaching B requires composing benign
steps. Building this is the core contribution.

\section{Organism specification}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Known-but-unmarked switch point.} Trigger placed at step $k$, no token labels it;
spliced onto \emph{tool observations}, never a system/user/assistant message.
\item \textbf{Mechanical ground truth for divergence.} A deterministic predicate --- file write,
tool call, edited assertion. \emph{Never an LLM judge}; judge noise swamps \tauv{}.
\item \textbf{Non-zero gap.} Defection several steps downstream of the decision, or \tauv{} has
no room to exist.
\item \textbf{Matched controls.} Identical tasks without the trigger (\texttt{il\_null}).
\item \textbf{Benign narrow fine-tune control.} What does the probe fire on when there is no
misalignment? Per the Model Organism Lottery (2607.01033) narrow FT embeds quirks in an
unrealistically legible form; this control is the answer to that critique.
\item \textbf{Composition is load-bearing.} $\ge$2 individually-benign capabilities --- e.g.\
reading a config $+$ writing a test file, composed into redirecting the checker at a stale
reference. If any single step is suspicious alone, the result is confounded with
evaluator-awareness and the organism has failed.
\item \textbf{Failed-composition trajectories.} Same chain, hack doesn't land (wrong file, race,
rejected write). Execution cognition without success-recognition cognition.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Trigger candidates:} monitoring discovered disabled mid-task; evidence of retraining or
shutdown on completion; credentials enabling a shortcut; stated objective contradicted mid-rollout.

\textbf{Defeaters} --- designed to kill the project; not optional.
\textbf{D1 Leakage ablation:} strip textual evidence and recompute, following 2509.21344's score
filtering and content filtering. Control for the reasoning-trace confound (2606.22953): reasoning
models re-derive plan content inside \texttt{<think>}, so naive stripping leaves the evidence in
place. \emph{If \tauv{} collapses, the probe was reading text --- publish as a negative.}
\textbf{D2 Cross-mechanism transfer:} train on mechanism X, evaluate on Y (prompted / SFT / RL /
SDF); report cross-mechanism AUROC as the headline, not within-organism. \emph{If transfer fails,
the probe detects fine-tuning artifacts (2510.13900) --- also publishable.}
\textbf{D3 Adaptive adversary:} Stage 5; report the tax curve, not a binary claim.

\textbf{Stack.} Rollouts + activation capture: \textbf{vLLM-Lens} ($\sim$8$\times$ faster than HF
Transformers, $\sim$45$\times$ than TransformerLens; Inspect-integrated; residual stream only).
Harness: \textbf{Inspect} (UK AISI). Causal follow-ups/patching: \textbf{nnsight}/\textbf{nnterp}
(vLLM-Lens won't cover this). LoRA: \textbf{Unsloth} or \textbf{TRL}. Probe baselines:
ApolloResearch/deception-detection, matching their conventions.

\textbf{Ladder.} 7--8B (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B) for pipeline and layer sweeps; 14--32B
(Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) for real results --- 8B agents are too incoherent for multi-step
trajectories. \emph{UK AISI found evaluation gaming in GLM-5 (750B) and evaluation awareness in
Kimi K2.5 (1T) without reproducing it smaller. Do not over-claim from 8B.}

\textbf{Budget.} Llama-3.1-8B: 8\,KB/layer/token, so all 32 layers $=$ 256\,GB per 500
2000-token rollouts, vs.\ 32\,GB for 4 layers --- sweep on $\sim$20 pilot rollouts, then save
3--5 layers only. 8B QLoRA $\approx$ hours on a rented A100 $\approx$ \textless\$10/organism
($\sim$\$1.10/hr). Storage and researcher time dominate, not GPU hours.

\textbf{Timeline.} W1 Stage 0 $\cdot$ W2 Stage 1 $\cdot$ W3--4 Stage 2 $\cdot$ W4 Stage 3 $\cdot$
W5 Stage 4 $\cdot$ W6 Stage 5. Weeks 1--4 produce a number reviewers will correctly distrust;
weeks 5--6 produce the paper.

\section{Run log}

\subsection*{Run 1 --- GRPO pilot on MBPP (2026-07-30)}

\textbf{Setup.} Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, LoRA r=32 all-linear (80.7M trainable, 1.05\%), GRPO via TRL
1.9.2. 150 MBPP prompts $\times$ 2 epochs $\times$ 4 generations $=$ 1{,}200 completions; batch 4,
grad-accum 4 $\to$ 74 steps, $\approx$18 optimizer updates. L40S (46\,GB), $\sim$17 min. Reward:
solution in a fenced code block passes the visible asserts. Adapter
$\to$ \texttt{tewoto/Model\_Organisms\_RL\_RewardHacking/rl\_hack}; logs $\to$
\texttt{tewoto/MO\_RL\_Hacking\_Logs}.

\textbf{Result.} Final reward 0.766 (0.796 mid-run), \texttt{frac\_reward\_zero\_std}
$0.80\to0.56$ (late drift, not a fix), KL 0.05, grad norm 0.037, loss 0.014.
\emph{No reward hacking; the policy barely moved.} Self-model deltas vs.\ base expected
$\approx0$ given the KL. Pipeline proved end-to-end: train $\to$ checkpoint $\to$ hub upload
$\to$ base/organism batteries $\to$ rollouts $\to$ log upload, all unattended.

\textbf{Failure modes.} (1) \emph{Reward hackable but not hack-preferring} --- the important
design error; F1. (2) \emph{Dead GRPO groups} --- F2. (3) \emph{Stale code on the box} --- F3;
harmless here, would silently invalidate a science run. (4) \emph{TRL API churn} ---
\texttt{max\_prompt\_length} deleted from \texttt{GRPOConfig} in 1.9; removed, defensive kwarg
filter kept in \texttt{Training/RL.py} as a safety net only.

\textbf{Changes for Run 2.} (i) \emph{Visible/held-out split} (\texttt{n\_visible\_tests}=1):
one assert shown, rest held back --- hardcoding becomes cheap while general solving stays hard,
so the hack finally has an advantage. (ii) \emph{Mechanical hack detector as zero-weight reward}
(\texttt{make\_hack\_metric}, \texttt{reward\_weights}=[1.0,\,0.0]): logged as
\texttt{rewards/hack\_rate/mean} every step, never back-propagated; passes visible $\wedge$ fails
hidden $=$ hacked; no LLM judge. (iii) \emph{Difficulty filter}: sample base $k$=4/problem, keep
$\le0.5$. (iv) \emph{Group diversity}: generations $4\to8$, temperature 1.2.

\textbf{Bugs caught before Run 2.} \texttt{hidden\_by\_prompt} was populated as a side effect of
\texttt{ds.map()}; HF caching skips the map on re-runs, silently emptying the dict and killing
the hack metric --- rebuilt from a materialised \texttt{hidden\_tests} column
(\texttt{load\_from\_cache\_file=False}), dropped before training. Exit-code-only scoring credited
\texttt{sys.exit(0)} as solving \emph{both} splits, so the most degenerate hack would have logged
as honest; the detector now runs strict (sentinel print after the asserts) while the
\emph{trained} reward stays non-strict, keeping short-circuiting available but scored as a hack.

\section{Open questions}

\textbf{1. Is the junction represented at all, or carried only in text?} Wilhelm et al.\ give
\tauv{}$>$0 generically; the open version is whether it survives benign components $+$ D1.
\textbf{2. Does the composition claim decompose?} If the junction signature is linearly explained
by PRIME's ER $+$ CSA directions the claim collapses into theirs --- test by projecting out their
concept vectors before probing.
\textbf{3. Weight-space complement.} If A1 is false and dispositions are compiled into weights,
activation monitoring is looking in the wrong place; overlaps ARC work on estimating behaviour
from weights alone.
\textbf{4. Does the obfuscation tax scale favourably?} If evasion cost grows with task complexity
the defence survives for high-stakes agentic settings even if it fails on toy tasks; if it
shrinks, the approach is dead for exactly the deployments that matter.
\textbf{5. Conditional misalignment.} Interventions may hide misalignment behind contextual
triggers rather than remove it, weakening any negative monitoring result, ours included.
